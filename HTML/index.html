Colosseum

Built by Vespasian and Titus from 72-82 AD, the Colosseum was the centre of showmanship for Rome. It hosted 50,000 to 80,000 spectators for public spectacles such as gladiatorial contests, public executions, and animal hunts.

Colossus Solis

Originally dedicated to Emperor Nero, after his death Vespasian added a crown and renamed it the Colossus Solis, after the sun god Sol.

Arch of Constantine

The Arch of Constantine was a triumphal arch dedicated to Constantine the Great, specifically to recognise Constantine's victory over Maxentius at the Battle at the Milvian Bridge in 312 AD

Temple of Venus and Roma

This temple was dedicated to the goddesses Venus Felix (Venus the bringer of good fortune), and Roma Aeterna, (Personification of the Roman State). It was started under Hadrian from 121-140 AD, finished by Antoninus Pius.

Meta Sudans Fountain

The Meta Sudans Fountain was built between 89-96 AD under the Flavian emperors. The name has Meta, meaning the shape the fountain took, and Sudans, meaning sweating. This could hint that the fountain did not jet out water, but rather oozed it out from the top.
