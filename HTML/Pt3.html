Temple of Venus Genetrix

This temple is dedicated to Venus Genetrix, (the mother), although it was originally intended to be dedicated to Venus Victrix (the victorious). These epithets are of the same goddess, however they encompass different parts of her identity.

Forum of Caesar

The forum of Caesar was originally meant to be an expansion of the Forum Romanum. It evolved to become a place for senate buseness, and the temple of Venus Genetrix. The land is said to cost 100,000,000 Sesterces, ~3 billion in military labour or daily goods.

Forum Romanum

The Forum Romanum (Roman Forum) was the centre of life in Rome. It held elections, triumphal processions, and public spectacles such as criminal trials, speeches, and gladiatorial matches. As a result, it also has many temples and basilica's built around it.

Comitium

The Comitium (Assembly) was an open air gathering place that held assemblies and orators. Citizens met here to vote people into power in the Roman Republic. It was largely dismantled by Caesar in the 1st century BC to make room for the Curia Julia (main senate house)

Arch of Severus Septimius

This was a triumphal arch dedicated in 203 AD to the victories that Severus Septimius and his sons won against the Parthians. Notably, the Severans built multiple triumphal arches.

Temple of Saturn

This temple was begun in the later years of the Roman Kingdom under Tarquinius Superbus, and its inauguration took place early in the Roman Republic, making it the second oldest Republican temple. Saturn ruled the golden age and was associated with wealth, so his temple served as a treasury, holding the Republic's gold and silver reserves.

Basilica Sempronia

The Basilica Sempronia was one of four original Forum Romanum basilica's, and the third built. It served as a public civic hall for legal business, law courts, and commercial gatherings. It was built by Tiberius Sempronius Gracchus, in 169 BC.

Basilica Aemilia

The Basilica Aemilia was built by Fulvius Flaccus and Aemilius Lepidus. It is the only surviving basilica from the Republican era in the Forum today. Many of the other basilica's were demolished or destroyed.

Temple of Janus

The temple of Janus is a symbolic place of beginnings, endings, and transitions. The god himself is two faced, representing two sides. The doors were referred to as the gates of war, open at war and closed during rare times of peace. Augustus made a political statement with this, closing the doors to signal a peaceful era after winning key battles.

Basilica Porcia

The Basilica Porcia was built by Marcus Porcius Cato (Cato the Elder). Similar to other basilica's, it served as an extension of the open-air forum, for conducting business. It was completely destroyed by a fire in 52 BC, that spread from Publius Clodius Pulcher's (Guy who snuck into women's only festival Bona Dea) funeral pyre.
