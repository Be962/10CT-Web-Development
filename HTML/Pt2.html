Cultural Storage

The Forum of Peace contained many cultural artefacts, including artefacts taken as spoils of war from Jerusalem, such as the seven-branched Menorah. It also had a library, called the Bibliotheca Pacis (library of peace).

Porticoes

The porticoes of the imperial fora were constructed out of exotic materials, such as marble, stone, and distinct decoration styles depending on the forum.

Temple of Minerva

The temple of Minerva honours the goddess of wisdom, justice, and crafts, Minerva, separate from the name of the forum itself. Minerva was the patron deity of Domitian, the emperor who started construction on the forum of Nerva.

'Ugly' Columns

The Forum of Nerva is the only imperial fora to lack a portico. Therefore they have these columns instead, due to the space constraints. The only surviving pair of columns are called 'le Colonnacce', literally translating to 'ugly columns' or 'bad columns'.

Temple of Mars Ultor

The Temple of Mars Ultor (Mars the Avenger) was built in 2 BC by Emperor Augustus. He vowed in 42 BC if Mars granted him victory in the battle of Philippi to build a temple to Mars.

Forum of Augustus

The Forum of Augustus (and the temple of Mars Ultor) was inaugurated in 2 BC, 40 years after Octavian vowed it. It provided an additional space beyond the Forum Romanum for legal proceedings.

Forum of Caesar

The forum of Caesar was originally meant to be an expansion of the Forum Romanum. It evolved to become a place for senate buseness, and the temple of Venus Genetrix. The land is said to cost 100,000,000 Sesterces, ~3 billion in military labour or daily goods.

Forum of Nerva

The Forum of Nerva is the smallest imperial fora. It is long and narrow, like a corridor. Fittingly, it served as a hub between the other major Roman forums, and worked like a large public passageway.

Forum of Peace

The Forum of Peace (or Vespasian's Forum) was built to commemorate the first Jewish-Roman war. It housed large amounts of art, serving as a public museum or art gallery, as well as a library that stored many important texts. It also had an open ornamental garden layout.
